What every football position demands: the physical traits that matter

By PlayerDNA Labs · 9 June 2026 · 6 min read

Footballers are not interchangeable. A flying winger and a commanding centre-back can have wildly different bodies, movement patterns and strengths — and the best players are usually the ones playing where their physical profile fits. Put a player in the wrong role and even real talent can look ordinary.

Here is the physical signature coaches and scouts look for in each position, and why it matters.

Goalkeeper

Modern goalkeeping is about explosive, repeatable reactions. The traits that matter most are reaction speed, vertical and lateral reach, and the agility to reset and dive again in a fraction of a second. Height and span help, but quick feet and fast decision-making separate the best from the rest.

Centre-back

Centre-backs win duels in the air and on the ground. Look for aerial power (vertical jump), strength in contact, and the recovery speed to cover space when the line is broken. Just as important is positional reading — being in the right place so raw pace is rarely tested.

Full-back & wing-back

This is arguably the most physically demanding role on the pitch. Full-backs need repeat-sprint stamina to get up and down the flank for ninety minutes, change-of-direction agility to defend 1v1, and the work rate to contribute at both ends. Endurance is the non-negotiable.

Defensive midfielder

The screen in front of the defence lives on reaction, positioning and endurance. Tackle timing and the ability to read danger early matter more than top speed — this is a role won with the brain as much as the body.

Central midfielder

Box-to-box midfielders need a bit of everything: stamina to cover the most ground of any outfield player, agility to turn in tight areas, and the decision speed to play quickly under pressure. Balance across many qualities — rather than one elite trait — defines the best in this role.

Attacking midfielder

The creator operates in the tightest spaces on the pitch. The key traits are agility, acceleration over short distances, and scanning — the awareness to know where everyone is before the ball arrives. Sharpness in the first two steps creates the time to make things happen.

Winger

Wingers are built on explosiveness. Look for top-end speed, acceleration, and the change-of-direction power to beat a full-back one-on-one. The ability to repeat those sprints — and stay dangerous late in games — is what turns a quick player into a match-winner.

Striker

Centre-forwards live on small margins. The defining traits are acceleration to get in behind, jump and strength to win and hold the ball, and the movement off the shoulder to time a run. Finishing is a skill — but the physical platform is what creates the chances in the first place.
